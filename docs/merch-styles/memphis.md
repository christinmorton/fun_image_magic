# Style Guide: Memphis, 1993–2001

The second style pack. Written 2026-11-08 from `resources/ghetto_asethetic_merch/`
(the Memphis covers, contact sheet and the regional timeline). **Status: draft.** Not in
the app yet.

---

## The look in one paragraph

Dark, grainy and a little dangerous. Memphis started in the tape underground: dubbed
cassettes with photocopied inserts, black-and-white photos shot in cemeteries and back
yards, typewriter text, and crude horror drawings. Red gothic letters drip like blood.
When the money came in, the covers went glossy like New Orleans, but stayed darker:
hellfire instead of gold palaces, toxic green and blood red instead of gold, masked
figures, a limousine next to a casket. Spooky and street, never pretty.

**Two looks in one pack:**
- **Tape era (1993–1996):** black-and-white, photocopied, handmade. The rawest look in the
  research.
- **Glossy era (1997–2001):** airbrushed collage like New Orleans, but darker and harder.

**Overall:** dark and low-key. Black first, then red or green. The tape era is almost
black-and-white, with one colour at most.

## The person

**Pose and framing**

- **Mean mug:** waist-up, staring straight at the camera, no smile, chin down a little.
- **Crouched:** squatting low in front of the scene (tape-era covers often did this).
- **Shadowed:** half the face in darkness, lit from one side or from below.

**Lighting:** low and hard. Red light from below for horror; flat flash for the tape era.

**Clothing options:**

- black hoodie, hood up
- oversized white tee
- dark work jacket
- bucket hat or skull cap
- plain bandana (black or white only: see Avoid)

**Jewellery options:** a gold chain, gold fronts, a pendant (a shape we design). Most tape-era
covers wore none, so "no jewellery" suits this pack.

## Lettering

New treatments this pack needs (the lettering engine has the New Orleans ones):

| Treatment | How it looks | How to build it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Blood** | Red gothic or horror letters with drips running off the bottom | A blackletter or horror font, a dark-to-bright red gradient, drips grown down from the letters' bottoms (random lengths, round ends), a black outline, a soft red glow |
| **Slime** | Thick toxic-green letters, glossy and dripping | The slab font with a green gradient, a strong bevel, a wet highlight, drips, and a dark green outline |
| **Typewriter** | Plain typed text, slightly uneven, as on a photocopied insert | A typewriter font, each letter nudged and inked a little differently, black on paper or white on black |

Also usable from New Orleans: **chrome** and **stone** for the glossy era.

**Font ideas** (all free for commercial use, to confirm when built): *UnifrakturMaguntia*
or *Pirata One* (blackletter), *Creepster* or *Nosifer* (dripping horror), *Special Elite*
(typewriter).

## Finish

- **Photocopy** (new finish, for the tape era): black-and-white, crushed blacks, blown
  whites, toner speckle, uneven exposure, a crooked edge, as if copied many times.
- **VHS** (existing) suits the glossy era's darker scenes.
- **Print** (existing) for the glossy era.

## Prompt pieces

**Base, glossy era (always used):**
> A square late-1990s Memphis underground rap album cover artwork: a dark, gritty,
> airbrushed digital photo collage. Low, hard lighting, deep shadows, mostly black with
> blood red and toxic green accents. Menacing and raw rather than luxurious. No text, no
> letters, no numbers, no logos, no stickers.

**Base, tape era (instead of the one above):**
> A square early-1990s underground rap cassette cover: a grainy black-and-white photograph,
> high contrast, shot with a flash on cheap film, as if photocopied. Raw and handmade. No
> text, no letters, no numbers, no logos, no stickers.

**Scenes:**

- **Graveyard at midnight:** The person stands among old leaning tombstones at midnight,
  with fog on the ground, bare twisted trees, crows and a full moon behind them. No fire or
  flames.
- **Hellfire:** The person stands on a cracked, burning landscape under a blood-red sky,
  with smoke, glowing embers and a crowd of shadowy figures behind them.
- **Smoke room:** The person stands in a dark room filled with thick smoke, lit only by red
  light from below, with candles on the floor around them.
- **Back street:** The person stands in an empty back street with a chain-link fence and
  power lines, on grainy black-and-white film. No fire or flames.
- **Limo and casket:** The person stands at night beside a long white limousine and a
  polished casket covered in flowers, lit like a king's funeral. No fire or flames.
- **Crew in the mist** (crews): The crew stands close together in thick fog, wearing hoods
  and caps, lit from below by red light.

**Person and layout space:** the same as New Orleans (`core/styles.py`).

## Product notes

- **Dark shirts suit this pack best;** the tape era's black-and-white also prints well on
  white shirts.
- **Thin drips and typewriter text** must still read at print size: keep drips thick and
  typewriter text large.

## Avoid

| Never include | Why |
| --- | --- |
| The Confederate flag (one cover in the research uses it) | A hate symbol to many; not for this line |
| Real artists, label names or logos | Their likeness and trademarks |
| The real Parental Advisory mark | A trademark |
| Real mugshots, booking numbers, police seals | Real records and official marks |
| Guns in hands or pointed at the viewer | Not right for merch |
| Gore | Horror here is mood (fog, graves, red light), not blood and wounds |
| Inverted crosses, pentagrams | Owner's call; left out by default, as they offend many buyers |
| Drug paraphernalia | Not right for merch |
