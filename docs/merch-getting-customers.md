# Merch App: Getting Customers

How to find buyers if the merch line starts with its own store instead of Etsy. A companion
to `docs/merch-app-plan.md`. Written 2026-11-02.
**Status: draft.** Brainstorm notes, nothing started.

---

## The short version

- **Your own store has no built-in shoppers.** On Etsy, people are already searching. On
  your own site, every visitor has to be brought there.
- **This product helps a lot.** A selfie turning into a 90s rap cover is something people
  stop to watch and want to share. The designs themselves are the advertising.
- **Most of the best ways cost time, not money:** short videos, events and customers
  sharing their own covers. Paid ads come later, once something is proven to sell.
- **Etsy can still run alongside it** (an Etsy shop is already connected through
  ProfitTree), for search traffic while an audience grows.

## Store first or Etsy first?

| | Your own store first | Etsy first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Where buyers come from** | Only from you: videos, events, word of mouth, ads | Etsy search, plus anything you bring |
| **First sale** | Slower: an audience has to grow first | Faster: buyers are already searching |
| **Fees** | Store subscription and payment fees | Etsy's listing, sale and payment fees |
| **Customer experience** | Can have the guided scan and live preview on the site | Text-only personalization, so photos arrive by message |
| **Rules** | Yours | Etsy's AI disclosure, production-partner and photo rules |
| **Customer list** | Yours to email again | Etsy limits how you can contact buyers |

## Ways to get customers, best first

### 1. Short videos (the main engine)

- **Where:** TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts.
- **Why it fits:** the before-and-after reveal is made for these feeds.
- **Ideas:**
  - "Turning my friends into No Limit-style album covers"
  - The scan itself: the head-turn guide, then the chrome name appearing
  - "Rate my crew's album cover"
  - Side-by-side of the four products: tee, hoodie, poster, phone case
  - A different region's style each week: New Orleans, Houston, Memphis, Compton
- **Cost:** time. Plan on posting often for several weeks before judging it.
- **Also tells you** which styles people like, before you spend money promoting them.

### 2. Events: the booth, again

- **The idea:** the guided scan and live preview set up at a table. People see their
  cover on the spot, order there, and you ship later.
- **Where:** pop-ups, block parties, car shows, hip-hop and throwback nights, college
  homecomings, barbershops, local markets.
- **Why it fits:** you've already built and run an event photo booth. This is the same
  thing with a product attached.
- **Good for crews:** groups come to events together, so it's the easiest place to sell
  crew designs.

### 3. Occasions where people buy for a group

Crew designs mean bigger orders, and these occasions already have a tradition of matching
shirts:

| Occasion | The pitch |
| --- | --- |
| **Birthdays** | "My 30th birthday album," with shirts for the whole party |
| **Family reunions** | The family as the crew on the cover. Custom reunion shirts are already a big market |
| **Bachelor and bachelorette parties** | The group as the crew |
| **Teams and clubs** | A team cover, with the roster as the "featuring" list |
| **Memorial tees** | A real tradition in this culture. Handle these personally and carefully, never as a sales push |

### 4. Let customers advertise for you

- **A free, watermarked preview** on the site. People post their cover, and every share is
  an ad.
  - Each preview costs about $0.05–0.20 in AI, so cap the free ones per person and ask for
    an email address first.
- **A referral discount**, which also nudges people toward crew orders.
- **Gifts to small local creators** who agree to it, such as DJs, barbers and local
  rappers. They post, and their followers see it.
  - Only with their consent, and never celebrities.
  - They must label the post as a gift or ad.

### 5. Paid ads (later)

- Meta and TikTok ads, run with whichever video already did best on its own.
- On a shirt that sells for about $35, ad costs can eat the profit. Only pay once organic
  posts have shown which video and style actually sell, and track the cost of each order.

### 6. Search (slow, but free once it works)

- Pages for what buyers type in, such as "custom 90s rap album cover shirt" or "family
  reunion rap cover shirt", on your site and on Pinterest.
- Expect months, not weeks.

## Starting simply

Store-first doesn't mean building the full website first. The cheapest start:

1. A basic store (for example Shopify) with the four products.
2. An order form that asks for **three photos** (front, left and right) plus the names
   and title.
3. You make each design by hand in the desktop app, send a proof, then order the print.

This is the same "cheap way" as in the app plan, just on your own store instead of Etsy.
The in-browser scan and live preview come later, with the API phase.

## What the app would need for this

Not in the app plan yet; add these to it if this route is chosen.

| Feature | For |
| --- | --- |
| **Watermarked preview export** | Free previews people can share |
| **A reveal video export:** the scan, then the cover appearing, as a vertical 9:16 clip | Short videos without screen-recording by hand |
| **Event mode:** quick order capture (name, email, product, size) at the booth | Selling at events |
| **Group orders:** one order, several people, several sizes | Crews, reunions, parties |
| **Preview limits per email** | Keeping free previews from costing too much |

## Rules

- **Get permission before posting anyone's face** in a video or ad, even friends.
- **No celebrities and no minors** in designs or marketing.
- **Gifted creators label their posts** as gifts or ads.
- **The album covers stay research only.** They never appear in videos, ads or previews.

## First steps

1. Make covers of yourself and a few friends who agree to it, and order real samples
   (a tee and a poster at least). These become the first videos and product photos.
2. Post short videos regularly for about a month. Note which styles and formats get shared.
3. Set up the simple store with the order form, and link it from every video.
4. Book one local event and run the booth with order-taking.
5. After the first month, look at the numbers below and decide whether to add Etsy,
   paid ads, or both.

## What to measure

| Number | Tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Views and shares per video | Which styles and formats spread |
| Store visits from each place (videos, events, referrals) | Where buyers actually come from |
| Previews made vs orders | Whether the preview convinces people |
| AI cost and ad cost per order | Whether each sale makes money |
| Average people per order | Whether crew designs are pulling their weight |

## Open questions

| Question | Leaning |
| --- | --- |
| Store platform: Shopify or a custom site? | Shopify to start, custom with the API phase |
| Run Etsy alongside from day one? | Worth it: it's free search traffic, and the shop already exists |
| Which platform to post on first? | Pick one and post consistently before adding others |
| First event | Something local and music-related |
